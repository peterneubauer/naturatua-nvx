Ustorp Nature Value Index

The Ocean Health Index method (ohicore, Baltic Health Index, Blekinge Health Index) applied on land, to the nature value biotopes mapped in the Swedish-standard inventories (NVI, SS 199000) of 2024 and 2026 at Eksjö Ustorp 1:6. Status is aggregated from indicators to three categories and on to NVX 0–100 and a nature value class. Pressures and resilience give a likely future state. Everything is aggregated area-weighted from biotope to BDU plot, BDU area, land parcel and the whole property.

The ambition

NaturaTua is completing its first Biodiversity Unit (BDU) transaction at Ustorp, putting biodiversity on a balance sheet as an asset. An asset needs a measure that can be repeated. The Swedish standard nature value inventory (NVI, SS 199000) is the ground truth here: a surveyor walks the land and assigns each biotope a nature value class from 1 (highest) to 4 (some). It is thorough, but it is done by hand, a few times per decade.

At the same time the project gathers sensor data continuously: acoustic recorders, ground-level panoramas, drone imagery, soil samples, eDNA, and the indicators AI can now extract from them. This page is an experiment in joining the two. It takes the Ocean Health Index method, built to normalise and aggregate many indicators into one score for marine assessments, and applies it on land to the NVI biotopes. The target is set by the surveyors: find the normalisations, weights and aggregations that make the computed class, NVX, land on the expert's NVI class for the biotopes we already have. Once the index reproduces the ground truth, the sensor-derived indicators can carry the classification between inventories, show trends year by year, and extend the same arithmetic to places and data types the NVI does not cover.

This is a two-hour test, not a finished method. The current agreement with the expert classes is shown in the chips above and the thresholds are recalibrated on every run. Since indicator extraction from raw data is now cheap, the same approach should work both on land and in the sea.

Method in brief

Status. Indicators are rescaled linearly between a zero point and a reference point (normalize_data). Sub-goals and categories are weighted arithmetic means (weighted_power_mean, p = 1). The three categories are combined into NVX with p = 0.5, so a weak category pulls the whole down, in line with the SS 199000 matrix where species value and biotope value interact.

Dimensions (ohicore::CalculateGoalIndex). Pressure: Σ(rank·value)/3, capped at 1. Resilience: weighted mean, never above pressure. Likely future: xF = (1 + β·trend + (1−β)·(resilience − pressure)) · status with β = 0.67. Score = (status + xF)/2. Trend is 0 until a time series exists.

Spatial aggregation (ohicore::CalculateAll, BHI SpatialIntersections). Every unit gets weighted.mean(score, area) for each branch and dimension over the biotopes it intersects, with the intersection area as weight. A unit's class follows from its aggregated status.

Class thresholds

The thresholds are chosen automatically to maximise agreement with the expert classes in NVI 2024/2026. This is a test of the method, not a replacement for the surveyor's judgement.

Gapfilling

Spatial gapfilling (GF-S01): a missing indicator is replaced by the median for biotopes of the same expert class. Objects 27–30 lack object sheets in the text export and are therefore largely gapfilled. eDNA is missing everywhere (delivery spring 2027) and counts as missing, not gapfilled.

The layers Structural variation (drone/3D), Connectivity (satellite), Soil carbon (soil samples) and Acoustic species richness (AudioMoth; the point positions are real, the species counts are not) are hypothetical, seeded random values (seed 2026), there to exercise point-to-polygon aggregation and gapfilling. They will be replaced by Conifer Vision's delivery, Sentinel-2, the soil samples and the 2025 acoustic analysis when available.

How the index is calculated

The Ocean Health Index (OHI) is a framework for combining many different measurements of an ecosystem into one comparable number between 0 and 100. It was developed for the world's oceans in 2012 and has since been adapted to the Baltic Sea (Baltic Health Index) and to the Blekinge archipelago (Blekinge Health Index). Here the same arithmetic is tested on land, with the nature value biotopes of a Swedish standard inventory as the smallest unit.

1. Indicator → score

Each indicator has a reference point (scores 1.0) and a zero point (scores 0). Between them the score is linear; outside it is clipped. Dead wood, for example, gets full score at 20 m³/ha, which corresponds to an old natural forest, and 0 at 0. The reference points are a first proposal, meant to be discussed with the surveyors.

2. Score → sub-goal → category

Indicators are weighted into sub-goals (e.g. Condition = naturalness ×2, dead wood ×1, habitat trees ×1) and sub-goals into three categories: Biotope value and Species value follow the two assessment grounds of SS 199000; Landscape & soil collects layers that the NVI does not measure but remote sensing, drones and soil sampling can provide.

3. Category → NVX → nature value class

The three categories are combined with a power mean (p = 0.5). That penalises imbalance: a biotope with high species value but low biotope value gets a lower NVX than the arithmetic mean would give, just as the SIS matrix places the assessment near its diagonal. NVX is translated into nature value class 1–4 (or 5, no nature value) with thresholds calibrated against the surveyors' own classes.

4. Pressures, resilience and likely future

OHI separates how things are now (status) from where they are heading. Pressures (felling, drainage, fertiliser, invasive species, overgrowth, buildings) are ranked 0–3 per category in a pressure matrix; resilience (active management, key-habitat or Natura 2000 status, connectivity) is weighted per category. Likely future = status × (1 + β·trend + (1−β)·(resilience − pressure)), β = 0.67, and the score is the mean of status and likely future. The 2026–2031 management agreement counts as resilience inside the BDU area, so the future holds up there despite high pressure from earlier felling.

5. From biotope to property

The biotopes are the reporting zones. Every larger unit (BDU plot, BDU area, parcel, property, habitat type, expert class) gets its value as the area-weighted mean of the biotopes it covers, for every branch and dimension. Sensor data that is reported on its own geometry (a recorder's 150 m footprint, a drone's 1 ha block) is attached to biotopes the way BHI's SpatialIntersections treats zones that do not line up with assessment units: a biotope's value is the mean over the footprints that cover it, weighted by intersection area, and the covered share is stored as a coverage figure. Aggregation upward uses biotope areas only, so a recording that covers three biotopes is never counted three times at BDU or property level. The share of the weight that was gapfilled or is missing is carried upward, so uncertainty is visible at every level.

6. Not included yet

Trends need at least two inventories; they are 0 here. Four layers are hypothetical until the real deliveries exist, and eDNA is missing entirely. The model is built to take in new indicators, including AI-derived ones, as one row in the index tree plus one value per polygon.
